# Unusual-tales storytelling

A method, not a source. Use it to build **original** stories. Never retell,
translate or closely paraphrase an actual 기기괴괴 episode — the plots are
copyrighted and the audience that knows the webtoon will call it out.

## What the source actually does (evidence)

Oh Seong-dae ran 기기괴괴 on Naver from 2013 to 2022 as an anthology of
self-contained stories. What reviewers, wikis and trope catalogues agree on:

- **One strange thing per story.** Each tale hangs on a single uncanny premise
  pulled from real life, social issues, viral trends or urban legend — a
  home plastic-surgery product ("Beauty Water"), a drawn switch that turns
  your family on and off ("Switch"), a ghost cleaning service ("Ghost Home
  Care"), a café loyalty card ("Cafe"), lottery numbers passed between
  strangers ("Winning Numbers").
- **Karma is the engine.** Most stories run on "what goes around comes
  around". Many protagonists are unpleasant on purpose, so the audience waits
  for the bill to arrive. The official blurb: the thing we should fear most
  is ourselves.
- **The twist is the payload, and it varies in kind.** Karmic punishment,
  an identity reveal, the protagonist was dead all along ("Delivery"), the
  truth was on the other side all along ("An African Incident": the
  "cannibals" were friendly, the guide invented it for revenge), the room
  was full of the same monster ("Grim Reaper": everyone in the suicide pact
  is a reaper), it-was-a-dream (used sparingly), and here-we-go-again loops
  ("The Stationery Store", "Winning Numbers").
- **Frightening by idea, not by gore.** Korean readers note it rarely relies
  on jump scares or disgust, so people who avoid horror still read it.
- **Not always horror.** Some tales go sad or sweet ("My Wife's Memories",
  "Dating a Ghost", "The Future Spirit"). The uncanny premise stays; the
  ending changes register.
- **Short, abrupt, eerie endings.** From late 2016 episodes got shorter and
  endings more bizarre and open. Readers split between "anticlimactic" and
  "like a Korean Junji Ito — no dragging".
- **One shared world.** Titles of other episodes appear as forum topics on
  in-story screens; one story is a stealth prequel to another ("The Makeover"
  → the surgeon invents Beauty Water).
- **Readers try to guess the ending in the comments.** The twist is designed
  to be deducible in hindsight, and the comment section became a guessing
  game.
- Grey, dark palette; clean, attractive character designs.

## The method, as rules

1. **One premise, stated as a rule, in the first line.**
   "มีแอปที่ฝากชั่วโมงไว้ใช้ทีหลังได้" — not a mood, a rule. If you need two
   sentences to explain the premise, it is two stories.
2. **The premise is mundane-adjacent.** A product, app, service, coupon,
   shrine, sticker pack. Something the viewer could plausibly own. The
   uncanniness is one twist on something ordinary, never a monster.
3. **Give the protagonist a want the premise feeds.** Vanity, laziness,
   greed, avoidance, grief. Decide early which mode you are in:
   - *Karma mode:* the protagonist is selfish in a way the audience
     recognises in someone they know. Show one small cruelty early so the
     ending feels earned, not sadistic.
   - *Grace mode:* the protagonist is sympathetic; the premise costs them
     something they only understand at the end.
4. **Escalate by repetition, in exactly three uses.** First use: a small
   delight. Second: a real benefit. Third: overuse — the moment they should
   have stopped. Viewers must see the rule applied, not hear about it.
5. **Plant the twist in plain sight twice.** One visual plant in the first
   third, one line of narration in the middle. On rewatch the ending must be
   deducible. This is what drives "I knew it" comments and rewatches.
6. **Pick the twist type deliberately** — and never the same type twice in a
   row across a series:

   | Type | Shape | Source example |
   |---|---|---|
   | กรรมตามทัน (karmic) | the benefit becomes the punishment, in the premise's own terms | Beauty Water |
   | ความจริงอยู่อีกฝั่ง (inverted truth) | who we feared was innocent; the guide was the threat | An African Incident |
   | ตายไปแล้ว (dead all along) | the protagonist's situation re-reads as an afterlife | Delivery |
   | ทุกคนคือสิ่งเดียวกัน (room of wolves) | the "others" share the protagonist's secret | Grim Reaper |
   | วนซ้ำ (it starts again) | the last beat hands the premise to the next person | Stationery Store, Winning Numbers |
   | ระวังสิ่งที่ขอ + ความฝัน (wish + wake) | use sparingly; only if waking changes behaviour | Switch |

7. **The twist reframes, it does not explain.** Land it in one image or one
   line inside the last 10 seconds. No epilogue explaining mechanics.
8. **Close on an aphorism or a hand-off.** Either one quiet line that names
   the theme without preaching (register: "เขาได้ทุกอย่างที่ขอ ยกเว้นคนที่จะ
   เล่าให้ฟัง"), or a hand-off: the rule passes to a new
   owner, which also makes the reel loop.
9. **Fear from the idea, not the edit.** No stingers, no jump cuts to faces.
   Let the viewer reach the horrible conclusion a half-second before the
   narration says it.
10. **Occasionally break genre.** One tale in four or five should end sweet
    or sad with the same uncanny premise. It keeps the anthology from
    becoming predictable, which is the one thing a twist anthology cannot
    survive.
11. **Build a shared world.** Let premises from earlier reels appear in the
    background of later ones — a forum thread title, an app icon, a flyer on
    a wall. Reward the viewers who watch everything.

## Mapping onto a 60–90s narrated vertical reel

| Time | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | the rule, spoken, over the object | 1, 2 |
| 3–12s | who wants it and why; one small cruelty or one sympathetic wound | 3 |
| 12–45s | use 1 → use 2 → use 3 (overuse); plant #1 visual, plant #2 spoken | 4, 5 |
| 45–70s | the cost starts arriving; the protagonist doubles down or tries to undo | 4 |
| last 10s | twist in one image or one line, then the aphorism or hand-off | 6, 7, 8 |

- The first frame shows the object, not the protagonist.
- Captions carry the rule and the twist line even with sound off.
- For comment-driven reach, the narrator may ask once, mid-story, "ถ้าเป็น
  คุณ จะใช้ครั้งที่สามไหม" — never "guess the ending", which spoils the plant.

## Pitch checklist

- [ ] Premise fits in one Thai sentence and is ownable/usable
- [ ] Want is clear; karma or grace mode chosen
- [ ] Three uses, third is overuse
- [ ] Two plants, both visible on rewatch
- [ ] Twist type named and different from the previous episode
- [ ] Last line is an aphorism or a hand-off, not an explanation
- [ ] No gore or jump scare doing the work the idea should do
- [ ] Original — not a retelling of any 기기괴괴 episode
